Let’s explore the pros and cons of using rice and paper confetti for weddings in New Zealand. Each option has its unique characteristics, and your choice will depend on your preferences, the wedding venue’s policies, and your commitment to sustainability.

## Rice Confetti

### Pros:

1. **Natural and Symbolic**: Rice has been used in weddings for centuries as a symbol of fertility, prosperity, and good fortune. Throwing rice signifies showering the couple with blessings for a fruitful life together.
2. **Budget-Friendly**: Rice is inexpensive and readily available. It won’t strain your wedding budget.
3. **Biodegradable**: Rice is a natural material that decomposes quickly, leaving no harmful residue behind.

### Cons:

1. **Clean-Up Challenge**: While rice is biodegradable, it can be challenging to clean up after the ceremony. Venues may hesitate to allow rice due to the mess it creates.
2. **Bird Safety Concerns**: There’s a myth that birds can eat rice and it will expand in their stomachs, causing harm. However, this myth has been debunked, and rice is generally safe for birds.

## Paper Confetti

### Pros:

1. **Colourful and Customisable**: Paper confetti comes in various colours, shapes, and sizes. You can match it to your wedding theme or choose a mix of vibrant hues.
2. **Lightweight and Photogenic**: Paper confetti floats beautifully in the air, creating stunning photo opportunities. It adds a whimsical touch to your wedding exit.
3. **Biodegradable Options**: Some paper confetti is made from biodegradable materials, making it an eco-friendly choice.

### Cons:

1. **Environmental Impact**: Not all paper confetti is biodegradable. Traditional paper confetti can take longer to break down, especially if it contains synthetic dyes or coatings.
2. **Venue Restrictions**: Many wedding venues in New Zealand prefer natural confetti (such as petals) to minimise environmental impact. Check with your venue before choosing paper confetti.
3. **Clean-Up Effort**: While paper confetti is lighter than rice, it still requires clean-up. Some venues may charge extra for confetti removal.

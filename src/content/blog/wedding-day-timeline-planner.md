A good wedding day timeline is the difference between a relaxed day and one spent chasing the clock. After photographing weddings across Auckland, I've seen the same pattern again and again: the days that feel calm aren't the ones with the most time. They're the ones where everyone knows what's happening next.

In this guide I'll share a sample timeline you can adapt, the timing mistakes I see most often, and how to make sure your bridal party, family and suppliers are all working from the same plan on the day.

The 20 minutes of congratulations after the ceremony doubles as your buffer. Something always runs over, and that slack is what keeps the rest of the day on time.

### **My top wedding timeline tips as a photographer**

**1. Give getting ready more time than you think.** Hair and makeup almost always run 15–30 minutes over, especially with a big bridal party. Schedule the person getting married to finish first or second, not last.

**2. Make a family photo list, and keep it short.** Each group takes 2–3 minutes once everyone is in place. Ten groups is about 25 minutes. Give the list to a confident family member or your MC so they can round people up, and tell everyone in advance to stay close after the ceremony.

**3. Plan around golden hour.** The best portrait light is roughly the hour before sunset. In Auckland that varies a lot through the year: sunset is around 8:40pm in early January, about 7:35pm in mid-October and as early as 5:10pm in June. For summer weddings that means slipping out during dinner for 15 minutes; for winter weddings, portraits often happen before the reception starts. Check the sunset time for your date and build around it.

**4. Account for travel.** If your ceremony and reception are in different places, add at least 15 minutes to whatever Google Maps says. Auckland traffic on a Saturday afternoon is unpredictable, and a bridal party takes a while to get into cars.

**5. Keep speeches together.** Two or three speeches in one block, with a time limit, keeps the night flowing. Speeches spread across the evening tend to stall the dance floor.

**6. Build in buffers and protect them.** Small 10–15 minute gaps around the ceremony and photos absorb delays. When things do run late, the key is telling everyone quickly.
